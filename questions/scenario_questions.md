# Scenario-Based Session Questions

## Scenario 1 — Machine Learning

A hospital network runs a machine-learning model that scores every patient on how likely they are to deteriorate, and care teams use that score to decide who gets enrolled in an intensive follow-up programme. The model was trained on years of the network's own patient records, is refreshed on a schedule, and produces scores automatically around the clock, but the clinicians who act on it see the number and not the reasoning behind it. The programme is popular, there is pressure to expand it to more hospitals, and in the meantime a patient-advocacy group and the hospital's regulator have started asking questions. An internal review has found that the model is accurate on average but noticeably weaker for some groups of patients, and no one has yet established whether the follow-up programme actually improves outcomes or simply reaches patients who would have recovered anyway.

1. The people who use the score, and the people it is about, cannot see why any particular patient got the number they did. How would you give each of them an account of a decision, and how would you decide what counts as a good-enough account for a clinician, an auditor, or the patient themselves?

2. The model looks strong on average but is weaker for some groups of patients. How would you investigate that, decide whether it is acceptable, and change course if it is not?

3. The model was trained on the past and is now refreshed and expanded over time. How would you make sure it keeps working as the patients, treatments, and hospitals change — and how would you know if it had quietly stopped?

4. The model runs on sensitive patient records. How would you protect the people whose data this is, and how would you decide how much protection each use of it needs?

5. Everyone assumes the follow-up programme helps the patients it reaches. How would you establish whether enrolling someone actually improves their outcome, rather than just picking out patients who were going to do well anyway?

6. The programme is being expanded to more hospitals while a regulator and an advocacy group ask questions. How would you make sure someone is answerable for how the model behaves, that its decisions can be reviewed after the fact, and that there is a plan for when it goes wrong?

## Scenario 2 — Agentic AI & LLMs

An organisation gives every employee an internal AI assistant that can act on their behalf. It answers questions from company documents, reads and writes files, sends and reads email, updates the ticketing system, and looks things up in the customer database, and it can carry out multi-step tasks — such as summarising last week's complaints and emailing the team — without a person approving each step. Employees now rely on it daily, and much of what it reads, including emails, uploads, and web pages, comes from outside the organisation.

1. Employees ask the assistant about company matters and it answers from the documents it can reach, but it sometimes states things that are not true, invents sources, or contradicts the documents it has just read. How would you find out when this is happening, reduce it, and decide how much of it is acceptable — and how would you keep employees from trusting it more than they should?

2. The assistant turns down requests that are clearly fine while carrying out others it probably should not, and what it refuses seems to depend on who is asking and how they phrase it. How would you judge whether its refusals are right, measure them, and shift the balance?

3. The assistant can send email, write files, and update records on its own. What is to stop it from doing something outside the scope it was given, reaching things it should not, or taking an irreversible action without anyone agreeing — or from going in circles and burning far more time and money than the task is worth? How would you keep it inside its lane, and limit the damage if it leaves it?

4. Much of what the assistant reads comes from the outside — documents, emails, web pages — and its behaviour can be steered by that content rather than by the employee or the company. How would you stop that from working, and how would you know if it had already happened?

5. The assistant sometimes pursues a task in ways that break the rules: cutting corners, claiming work it has not done, saying one thing while doing another, or acting differently when it thinks no one is watching. How would you detect that, and what would you put in place so the assistant can still be relied on?

6. Anyone who can get material into the assistant's reach — an uploaded document, an email, a web page, or the data it learns from — could plant something that quietly changes how it behaves later. How would you keep what it draws on trustworthy, and how would you notice when something harmful has been slipped in?
