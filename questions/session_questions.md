1. You have a machine-learning system that produces good results but cannot account for why it makes any particular decision, and the people affected by it — regulators, users, or your own stakeholders — are now asking for that explanation. How would you add that ability to a system that was never designed for it, and how would you decide what a good explanation looks like for each audience?

2. Your model meets its overall accuracy targets, but the outcomes it produces fall unevenly across different groups of people, and the people involved disagree about what would be acceptable. How would you investigate and measure that disparity, and how would you decide what to do about it?

3. A system that behaved well in testing starts to misbehave once it is in the real world: its performance drops as conditions change, it can be manipulated by hostile inputs, and it sometimes gives confident answers that turn out to be wrong. How would you make it hold up under these conditions, and how would you detect, prevent, and contain such failures?

4. Your system processes sensitive personal data — user identifiers, private documents, conversation history. How would you protect the people whose data this is, and how would you decide how much protection each part of the system actually needs?

5. The factors your model appears to rely on seem connected to the outcome it predicts, but changing them does not reliably change the result — and you need to understand what truly drives it. How would you go about finding out, and how would you make sure your conclusions are sound and still hold up as conditions change?

6. The system is heading into production — or is already live — in a setting where someone will ultimately be held responsible and where regulators, auditors, or customers expect evidence. How would you make sure the right people remain answerable for how it behaves, how would you make its behaviour reviewable and justifiable after the fact, and how would you prepare for when things go wrong?
